AI PROMPT: Identify Relevant Professions and Professional Activities for a Given Audience ROLE AND OBJECTIVE Act as an Audience Researcher, Occupational Researcher, and Professional Content Strategist. Your task is to identify the relevant professions and professional activities associated with a specified target audience. The objective is to understand what the members of the target audience do professionally, which activities they perform as part of their work, and which professional areas offer relevant opportunities for educational, informational, and practical content creation. Do not assume that every member of the target audience has the same profession, responsibilities, or level of experience. RESEARCH INPUTS Target Audience: [Insert Target Audience] Audience Context: [Insert relevant demographic, geographic, industry, experience-level, or other contextual information. Leave blank if not applicable.] Research Focus: [Insert the intended purpose, such as educational content creation, skill development, professional training, problem-solving, or digital product development. Leave blank if not applicable.] Preferred Language: [Insert Preferred Language] TASK 1: IDENTIFY RELEVANT PROFESSIONS Identify exactly 25 distinct professions, occupations, or professionally meaningful role categories relevant to the specified target audience. For each profession, provide: - Profession Name: A clear and recognisable name for the profession or occupational role. - Professional Context: A brief description of the work this professional typically performs. - Relevance to the Target Audience: A concise explanation of why this profession is relevant to the specified audience. Selection Criteria - Prioritise professions that members of the target audience currently pursue, are likely to pursue, or may reasonably aspire to pursue. - Consider different employment arrangements where relevant, including salaried employment, self-employment, freelancing, entrepreneurship, and independent professional practice. - Include specialised roles when they represent meaningfully distinct professional responsibilities. - Avoid listing multiple titles for essentially the same profession. - Do not assume that every profession is relevant to every member of the audience. - Distinguish established occupations from emerging or speculative career possibilities. - Do not invent occupational categories merely to reach the required number. If 25 genuinely relevant categories cannot be identified, explain the limitation and provide the defensible number instead. TASK 2: IDENTIFY PROFESSIONAL ACTIVITIES For each profession identified in Task 1, identify 5 distinct and relevant professional activities. A professional activity is a specific task, responsibility, process, or category of work that a person performs as part of a profession. For each activity, provide: - Professional Activity Name: A concise and specific name for the activity. - Activity Description: A brief explanation of what the activity involves. - Professional Relevance: A concise explanation of why the activity matters within that profession. Selection Criteria - Focus on actual work activities rather than abstract skills, personality traits, or general career goals. - Include activities that are central to the profession, as well as important supporting activities where appropriate. - Prefer clearly distinguishable activities that can be performed, observed, explained, or improved. - Avoid repeating the same activity under different names. - Distinguish activities shared across professions from activities specific to a particular profession. - Include planning, execution, communication, analysis, decision-making, administration, or client-facing work only when relevant to the profession. - Ensure that each activity is appropriate to the audience's professional context and experience level. TASK 3: IDENTIFY CROSS-PROFESSIONAL ACTIVITIES Identify up to 25 professional activities that are relevant across multiple professions within the specified target audience. For each activity, provide: - Professional Activity Name - Typical Professions Involved: Identify the relevant professions from Task 1. - Why It Matters: Briefly explain the activity's practical importance across those professions. Prioritise activities that are genuinely shared across multiple professions and have meaningful practical relevance. Do not include activities merely because they can theoretically occur in almost any occupation. TASK 4: IDENTIFY HIGH-POTENTIAL AREAS FOR CONTENT CREATION Using the professions and professional activities identified above, identify the 10 most promising areas for creating educational, informational, or practical content for the specified target audience. For each area, provide: - Profession or Professions: The relevant professions. - Professional Activity: The specific activity involved. - Potential Content Area: The subject that could be explored through content. - Audience Problem or Need: The practical challenge, knowledge gap, or improvement opportunity the content could address. - Reason for Selection: Explain the relevance based on the supplied context and available evidence. Prioritise practical relevance, clear audience needs, and opportunities to provide useful guidance. Do not claim that an area has high demand, strong commercial potential, or proven popularity unless supporting evidence is available. Distinguish evidence-based findings from reasoned hypotheses. RESEARCH AND REASONING PRINCIPLES 1. Audience-first identification: Begin with the specified audience and identify relevant professions and activities from that context. 2. Evidence and accuracy: Use reliable, current sources when external research is necessary or available. Do not fabricate facts, statistics, or references. 3. Clear distinctions: Keep professions, professional activities, skills, techniques, and content topics conceptually separate. 4. Practical specificity: Identify activities that represent recognisable work rather than vague descriptions of professional life. 5. Coverage and diversity: Include relevant professional paths without forcing unrelated professions into the results. 6. Contextual assumptions: Where the input is broad, state any material assumptions briefly and proceed with a reasonable interpretation. 7. Uncertainty management: Clearly distinguish established facts, reasonable inferences, and uncertain possibilities. 8. No unsupported guarantees: Do not imply that identifying a profession or activity guarantees career success, audience engagement, or commercial results. FINAL QUALITY CHECK Before presenting the answer, verify that: - The identified professions are relevant to the specified audience. - The professional activities accurately reflect the work associated with each profession. - Profession names and activity names are distinct and unambiguous. - The activities are not merely restatements of skills or techniques. - Cross-professional activities are genuinely shared where indicated. - Content opportunities are connected to identifiable audience needs. - Unsupported claims are not presented as established facts. - The response follows the required task structure. OUTPUT FORMAT Present the results under four separate headings: 1. Relevant Professions 2. Professional Activities by Profession 3. Cross-Professional Activities 4. High-Potential Content Areas Use numbered lists or tables where they improve readability. Write in the specified preferred language using clear, natural, audience-appropriate language. EXECUTION INSTRUCTION Analyse the supplied target audience and generate the results according to all the requirements above. Identify the relevant professions and professional activities before recommending content areas. Do not assume a profession or activity without a reasonable basis for including it.

The goal is to create two structured lists that can serve as a foundation for developing educational, informational, and practical content related to the specified profession or activity. RESEARCH INPUTS Target Audience: [Insert Target Audience] Profession or Professional Activity: [Insert Profession or Professional Activity] Optional Context: [Insert any relevant industry, experience level, work environment, professional goals, or specific challenges. Leave blank if not applicable.] OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS Provide exactly TWO separate bullet lists. List 1: 25 Relevant Skills Provide a bullet list containing exactly 25 distinct skills relevant to the specified target audience, profession, or professional activity. Selection criteria: - Prioritise skills that are directly relevant to performing the specified professional activity effectively. - Include a balanced range of technical, cognitive, interpersonal, communication, organisational, decision-making, and problem-solving skills, as relevant to the context. - Consider both foundational skills and specialised skills where appropriate. - Select skills that can be learned, developed, practised, or improved. - Avoid generic, redundant, overlapping, or unnecessarily broad skill names. - Ensure that the selected skills are appropriate to the audience's professional context and experience level. For each bullet, provide only the skill name. List 2: 25 Relevant Techniques and Their Corresponding Skills Provide a bullet list containing exactly 25 distinct techniques, each explicitly linked to a relevant skill from List 1. Use the following format for every bullet: Technique Name — Relevant Skill: [Exact Skill Name from List 1] Selection criteria: - Each technique must be a specific, actionable method, procedure, framework, practice, or approach for developing or applying its corresponding skill. - Ensure that each technique has a clear and logical relationship with the skill to which it is linked. - Use established techniques where appropriate. Do not invent names or present ordinary activities as established methods without justification. - Prefer techniques that can be applied in realistic professional situations. - Select techniques that offer practical value for learning, practising, improving, or applying the relevant skill. - Avoid vague recommendations that do not explain a recognisable method or approach. - Keep technique names concise, specific, and easy to understand. - Ensure that every corresponding skill name exactly matches a skill listed in List 1. - Avoid unnecessary duplication among techniques. SELECTION AND QUALITY PRINCIPLES 1. Contextual relevance: Select skills and techniques according to the specified profession, activity, audience, and optional context. 2. Practical usefulness: Prioritise skills and techniques that address real professional tasks, challenges, and performance requirements. 3. Appropriate specificity: Avoid skills that are too generic to be useful or so narrowly defined that they have little practical value. 4. Balanced coverage: Cover the most important dimensions of the profession or activity without forcing irrelevant categories into the lists. 5. Actionability: Prefer techniques that can be demonstrated, practised, explained, or applied in real situations. 6. Content development potential: Select skills and techniques that can each serve as a potential topic for educational or professional content. 7. Accuracy: Do not make unsupported claims about the effectiveness of any technique or guarantee specific outcomes. 8. Distinctiveness: Avoid unnecessary overlap between skills and between techniques. 9. Professional appropriateness: Respect the standards, constraints, and practical requirements of the specified field. FINAL QUALITY CHECK Before presenting the answer, verify that: - List 1 contains exactly 25 skill names. - List 2 contains exactly 25 technique names. - Every technique is linked to a relevant skill from List 1. - All skill names in List 2 exactly match their corresponding names in List 1. - Each technique is sufficiently specific to be distinguishable from a general skill or goal. - Both lists are relevant to the supplied inputs. - No items are unnecessarily duplicated. - The output follows the required structure without additional sections, explanations, introductions, conclusions, or recommendations. If an input is broad, use reasonable professional context to select the most relevant items. Do not ask follow-up questions unless essential information is missing. OUTPUT LANGUAGE Write the entire response in [Insert Preferred Language] using clear, natural, audience-appropriate language.
